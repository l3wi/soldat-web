## Implemented contract

- One Node 24 `worker_thread` and one real WASM instance per match. Fixed 60 Hz monotonic accumulator; at most four catch-up steps per wake, with discarded debt/overload counters. No client packet buys simulation time.
- Admission verifies wire/content/ABI identities, nickname and legal loadout. Joins explicitly select `control:"authoritative"`; primary and secondary next-respawn choices are recorded in the sim. Clients send quantized intent, never position, velocity, damage or hit claims. The listener remains WSS-only; local qualification uses a real self-signed certificate.
- Server arrival lead is **at most eight queued ticks**, per the explicitly revised SPEC §4.4; the bounded per-player queue holds at most nine target ticks and consumes each only at its fixed tick. Inputs are sequenced, newest plus two previous frames, validated oldest first, with a 128-sequence jump ceiling. Both listener and worker retain the 90-packet/s token bucket and burst 128. Gaps repeat movement/fire/jets/aim but clear action bits 6–11; after 15 silent ticks held controls become neutral. Resync preserves the accepted sequence floor and permits one forward sequence rebase. Buffer occupancy and queued lead are measured, not inferred.
- Ordinary snapshots run at 30 Hz. Deltas use only this recipient's acknowledged retained baseline. Each includes the authoritative tick's `sim_hash_state` and consumed remote controls. Candidate overflow resets to fullstate. WSS carries **one outstanding fullstate** until its exact ACK is accepted by the worker; it does not enqueue repeated bulk fullstates while waiting. Full bootstrap/resync ACK has a 60-second deadline. RTT probes start only after synchronization, pause across fullstate recovery, retain their genuine timestamp, and resume with a fresh 10-second response deadline. Repeated pending resync requests coalesce.
- Canonical simulation gameplay events `0x1001..0x1007` carry death, flag information, Thing pickup/state, respawn, team scores and match end. Raw legacy packet events and presentation events are not forwarded as reliable gameplay. Confirmation IDs are assigned after the 12-tick rewind window, retained until cumulative ACK, retransmitted, and deduplicated on the client. Event overflow explicitly closes the session; it does not silently discard a kill or capture.
- Client online mode is `?server=wss://…&map=Arena`. Both prediction and extraction hosts verify the frozen module and full raw content manifest. Snapshot reconciliation loads authoritative fullstate, compares hashes **before replay**, and replays unacknowledged local controls with `REPLAY`; remote controls are held with one-shot actions cleared. The input ring is 128 frames, with full resync on overflow. Rendering corrections never modify gameplay memory.
- Remote soldier poses come from a **second real WASM instance**, loaded but never stepped for extraction. Sixteen authoritative pose snapshots interpolate from 50 ms, adapting within 33–100 ms. The local soldier uses prediction and an 80 ms presentation-only correction decay. Skeleton pose-ring storage is about 192 KiB, in addition to the extra host. This avoids brittle parsing of opaque Pascal state layouts. Extraction/reconciliation costs are exposed for measurement, not assumed free.
- Acknowledged client baselines remain pinned until a later server baseline watermark proves them obsolete. Bounds are 128 candidates/64 MiB; capacity withholds new ACKs instead of evicting an eligible baseline. Initial fullstate bursts and later unsolicited fullstate resets are distinguished. Only one recovery request is outstanding; queued old-epoch deltas cannot reopen prediction before a fullstate arrives.
- Debug display reports RTT, snapshot-ID gaps (not raw TCP packet loss), correction, interpolation, same-tick server/client hashes, and estimated generation-to-now snapshot age. Receipt age is also shown; RTT/2 age assumes symmetric latency. Rig metrics preserve receipt and estimated age separately.

### Projectile policy and user-visible trade-off

The server retains 31 tick-boundary history entries spanning 500 ms, consumed controls and historical weapons. Normal ticks retain fullstate anchors. Delayed Fire/ThrowNade intent can rewind at most `min(12,ceil(RTT_ms*0.03)+2)` ticks, with further view-tick validation against server-measured RTT. Only fire/grenade intent and aim are inserted; recorded movement is not rewritten. Every player's recorded controls are resimulated to now using `REPLAY`; pending gameplay events are recomputed. Overlapping rewinds invalidate affected later state anchors and replay from the nearest still-valid retained anchor rather than serializing/hash-copying every replayed tick. Admission still limits the inserted shot's catch-up to 200 ms; replay may include an earlier already-corrected prefix. Already committed prefix events are drained, never confirmed again. Metrics count actual replayed steps, including that prefix. Membership changes invalidate history. Work is bounded to one rewind per current match tick and one per player per six ticks.

Bullets/pellets/arrows/flame, M79 and LAW use the same historical projectile/target timeline; they are not converted to hitscan. Grenades preserve original animation, launch, fuse, bounce and explosion semantics. Genuine subsequent held controls are required where original startup/throw animation needs them; unreceived continuous hold is not fabricated. Delayed knife/chainsaw/unarmed Fire is rejected, including grenade-plus-Fire attempts to bypass that rule. Timely melee runs normally.

Fullstate resimulation preserves coherent projectile/explosion interactions compared with target-only rewind, without a second authoritative server instance. It costs state validation and real replayed steps; the host no longer serializes/copies each intermediate replay boundary. **Confirmed kill-feed/score notifications arrive approximately 200 ms plus downstream delivery time later**; predicted movement/audio/cosmetics remain immediate. This deferral prevents a historical shot from retracting an already-delivered confirmation.
